# Proscene SEO Strategy: Blog & Documentation

*A complete organic traffic playbook for proscene.app — built around the blog ("Notes from the Prompt Desk") and a new manual/tutorial section. Prepared June 2026.*

***

## Executive Summary

Proscene currently has zero organic search presence. That is a problem, but it is also an opportunity: no negative history, no penalties, and an entirely open field in a niche where competitor SEO is weak. The strategy below builds organic traffic through two distinct but connected channels — a **craft-focused blog** that attracts stage managers and theatre professionals through informational content, and a **product documentation hub** that captures high-intent "how do I" queries from existing and prospective users. Together they create a flywheel: blog content builds authority and audience, docs convert that audience into active users, and internal links connect the two.[^1]

The niche is small but the search intent is sharp. A stage manager Googling "how to write a rehearsal report" or "theatre production scheduling app" is precisely the target buyer. The goal is to own every meaningful search in that lane.

***

## Part 1: Understanding the Keyword Landscape

### The Three Intent Tiers

All SEO traffic for Proscene falls into three buckets, each requiring different content:

| Tier | Intent | Example Queries | Best Content Type | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Craft/skill-building; not product-aware | "how to write a rehearsal report," "blocking notation guide," "tech week checklist SM" | Blog posts, guides | Build audience; establish authority |
| **Consideration** | Evaluating tools and workflows | "best stage management software," "theatre production app," "ProductionPro alternative," "StageWrite vs [X]" | Comparison pages, feature landing pages | Capture mid-funnel; convert to trial |
| **Activation** | Already using or onboarding Proscene | "how to set up blocking in Proscene," "Proscene rehearsal report tutorial," "how to import cast list" | Docs / manual pages | Reduce friction; drive feature adoption |

The blog targets **Tiers 1 and 2**. The documentation/manual targets **Tier 3** primarily, but well-structured docs pages also rank for Tier 1 and 2 queries over time.[^2]

### Realistic Volume Assessment

This is a niche market. Individual keyword volumes are modest — often 100–1,000 monthly searches per term. The value is not in any single high-volume keyword but in **owning the entire cluster**. A stage manager who finds a Proscene blog post while Googling "rehearsal report template," bookmarks it, reads two more posts, and then signs up for the product is more valuable than 10,000 generic visitors. Target depth of relevance over breadth of volume.

## Part 2: Blog Strategy — "Notes from the Prompt Desk"

### The Blog's Role in the SEO Strategy

The blog has one job at this stage: be the best free resource for stage managers on the internet on the topics Proscene owns. Not a product newsletter. Not a press release hub. A genuine craft resource that earns trust, builds backlinks organically, and captures Tier 1 and Tier 2 search intent.

The brand already has a compelling content identity — "Notes from the prompt desk, by people who've held the book". Every post should feel like it came from that place: insider, practical, fluently theatre-literate, and useful even if the reader never signs up for Proscene.[^1]

### Content Architecture

Organize the blog into **four content pillars**, each mapping to a keyword cluster and a stage of the SM career:

**Pillar 1 — The Craft (Tier 1 / awareness)**
How-to guides and deep dives on SM technique. These posts exist to rank for "how to" queries and build long-term authority. They should be comprehensive, referenceable, and linkable.

**Pillar 2 — The Toolkit (Tier 2 / consideration)**
Posts that compare approaches, tools, and workflows — honest evaluations that naturally position Proscene. These capture searchers who are actively evaluating software.

**Pillar 3 — The Production** (Tier 1 / awareness + community)
Production-phase content: prep week, rehearsal room, tech week, run, strike. Checklist-style posts that stage managers will bookmark and share.

**Pillar 4 — For Theatre Companies** (Tier 2 / consideration)
Broader organizational content for producers, directors, and theatre admins — the economic buyers for Proscene's org plans.

### On-Page SEO Standards for Every Blog Post

- **Title tag**: include the primary keyword naturally; keep under 60 characters
- **Meta description**: 120–155 characters; include primary keyword + a clear value statement
- **H1**: matches or closely mirrors the title tag
- **H2/H3 structure**: each sub-topic gets its own heading; headings use secondary keywords naturally ("rehearsal report sections," "blocking notation symbols," etc.)
- **Internal links**: every post links to at least 2–3 other blog posts and 1–2 relevant documentation pages
- **CTA**: every post has one primary CTA — either "See how Proscene handles this →" (linking to a relevant feature or docs page) or "Start your free trial"
- **Schema markup**: add `Article` schema to all blog posts; add `HowTo` schema to checklist/tutorial posts — this increases eligibility for rich snippets[^2]

## Part 3: Documentation / Manual Strategy

### Why a Manual Section Is SEO-Critical (Not Just UX)

Product documentation is one of the most underutilized SEO channels in SaaS. Most companies treat docs as a support cost. Docs pages match the highest-intent query pattern that exists: "how to do [specific thing] in [specific product]." Someone searching that query has already decided to try the product — they just need to succeed with it.[^2]

Additionally, well-structured docs pages rank for broader "how to" queries too. A Proscene tutorial on "How to set up blocking in Proscene" also competes for "how to set up blocking theatre software" — capturing new users who haven't heard of Proscene yet.

### Recommended URL Structure

Keep docs on the same domain (`proscene.app/docs/` or `/manual/`), never on a subdomain (`docs.proscene.app`). A subdomain splits domain authority — all the SEO value the site earns will not flow to the docs. Same domain, same path.[^2]

### Documentation Page Types and SEO Purpose

Each page type serves a different query intent:[^2]

**1. Overview / Hub Pages** (`/docs/blocking/overview/`)
- Introduce the feature area, explain what it does and who it's for
- List 5–7 "most common tasks" as internal links
- Target: "[feature] in theatre software," "what is [feature] Proscene"
- Keep these indexable and well-linked from the main site nav

**2. Setup / How-To Pages** (`/docs/blocking/setting-up-your-ground-plan/`)
- Step-by-step task completion with screenshots
- Target: "how to [task] in Proscene," "Proscene [feature] setup"
- These also rank for generic "how to [task] theatre software" queries
- Format: numbered steps, each with a screenshot; include expected result; include one "If you see this problem, here's the fix" note

**3. Concept Pages** (`/docs/people/roles-and-permissions/`)
- Explain the "why" behind a design decision (e.g., why there are 8 roles, what each role can do)
- Target: "theatre software user roles," "stage management permissions"
- Link from here to the corresponding how-to pages

**4. Troubleshooting Pages** (`/docs/troubleshooting/invites-and-access/`)
- Problem-first format: symptom → cause → fix
- Use the exact error message or symptom as the H1 (e.g., "My invite link expired — what do I do?")
- These capture the most distressed, highest-intent searchers[^2]
- Link back to the relevant setup guide

### Priority Documentation Pages (Build These First)

These are the pages that will get the most traffic and reduce the most support questions:

1. **Getting Started Overview** — the single most important page; every new user lands here
2. **Set Up Your First Production (Setup Wizard walkthrough)**
3. **Invite Your Company (how the one-link invite works; how roles get assigned)**
4. **Importing a Cast List from CSV** — high search volume query; bridges to blog post #10
5. **Creating and Sending Calls** — core daily workflow; linked from blog posts on scheduling
6. **How to Capture Blocking (Beat by Beat)** — supports the blocking blog post
7. **Creating and Distributing a Rehearsal Report** — the flagship feature; deserves the most thorough treatment
8. **AI Script Analysis: Uploading and Reviewing** — the marquee differentiator; needs clear documentation
9. **Roles and Permissions Explained** — answers the most common "why can't I see X" questions
10. **Troubleshooting: Invite and Access Issues** — the most common support touchpoint for new companies

### On-Page Standards for Docs Pages

- **Title tag format**: "[Task] — Proscene Help" (e.g., "How to Create a Rehearsal Report — Proscene Help")
- **H1**: matches the task exactly as someone would search it
- **First paragraph**: one sentence stating what this page covers and who it's for
- **Steps**: numbered, one action per step, screenshot or visual for each
- **"Next steps" block**: every page ends with 2–3 links to the logical next actions
- **Breadcrumb navigation**: `/Docs / Reports / Creating a Rehearsal Report` — this helps Google understand hierarchy[^2]
- **Schema**: add `HowTo` schema to all step-by-step docs pages

## Part 4: Internal Linking Architecture

Internal links are the connective tissue that makes both channels work together. A stage manager who finds the rehearsal report blog post should have a clear, low-friction path to the Proscene docs page, and from there to the trial signup.

### The Three-Link Loop

Every piece of content should participate in this pattern:

```
Blog Post (Awareness)
  → Docs Page (How-to, specific to Proscene)
    → Feature/Pricing Page (Conversion)
      → Trial Signup
```

**Example:**
- Blog: "How to Write a Rehearsal Report" → includes a CTA: "See how Proscene auto-assembles reports"
- That link goes to: `/docs/reports/creating-a-rehearsal-report/`
- The docs page includes a sidebar or footer CTA: "Start your 60-day free trial"

### Key Internal Linking Rules

- **Every blog post** links to at least one docs page and one feature page
- **Every docs overview page** links to the corresponding feature page on the marketing site
- **The marketing feature pages** link to their most relevant docs pages (proof of capability)
- **Use descriptive anchor text**: "how to capture blocking in Proscene" not "click here"
- **Never link docs to the homepage**: link to the specific feature page or pricing page instead[^2]
- **Create a "Related articles" block** at the bottom of every docs page with 3–4 cross-links

## Part 5: Technical SEO Foundations

These are table-stakes items that must be in place before any content is published. Given the Next.js/Vercel stack, most of these are straightforward to implement.[^1]

### Crawlability and Indexing

- Verify `robots.txt` allows crawling of `/docs/` and `/blog/` — do not accidentally block with a wildcard rule
- Confirm no accidental `noindex` tags on blog or docs pages (easy mistake when building in Next.js)
- Submit XML sitemaps for both `/blog/` and `/docs/` to Google Search Console on day one
- Use clean, stable URLs — no query parameters, no session IDs, no auto-generated slugs

### Structured Data (Schema Markup)

| Page Type | Schema to Add |
|---|---|
| Blog posts | `Article` |
| How-to blog posts | `Article` + `HowTo` |
| Docs how-to pages | `HowTo` |
| Docs troubleshooting | `FAQPage` (if structured as Q&A) |
| Pricing page | `SoftwareApplication` |
| Homepage | `SoftwareApplication` + `Organization` |

### Page Speed

Proscene runs on Vercel with Next.js — Core Web Vitals should be strong by default. Confirm with PageSpeed Insights after launch. The one risk area is the blog if images are not properly optimized (use `next/image` with proper sizing).[^1]

### The "Proscene" Brand SERP

Right now a search for "Proscene" returns nothing about the product. Once the site is indexed, claim:[^1]
- Google Business Profile (yes, even for SaaS — it anchors the brand SERP)
- Set up Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools immediately
- Create a Wikipedia-style "About" or crunchbase-style profile page to seed brand knowledge

## Part 7: Content That Cannot Be Faked

The single biggest advantage Proscene has over a generic content marketing agency is authenticity. The target audience — working stage managers — can immediately detect when content is written by someone who has never held the book. Every blog post should be written in the voice of a working SM, using real production language:

- Use theatre-specific terminology without explanation: "stumble run," "cue to cue," "the half hour," "load-in," "the book," "prompt desk"
- Reference real production paperwork formats (USITT, Yale SM program standards)[^4]
- Acknowledge the real constraints: volunteer casts, community budget, directors who change blocking on the fly, cast members who ignore group chats
- Link to real SM community resources (SMNetwork, USITT, Bard College SM templates) — this builds credibility and earns reciprocal links over time[^19]

This voice is already embedded in the Proscene brand. The blog just needs to use it consistently, at volume.
